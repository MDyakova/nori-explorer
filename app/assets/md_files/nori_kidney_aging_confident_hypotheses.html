Detailed hypotheses

1. Spatially heterogeneous / focal kidney aging

Hypothesis. Kidney aging does not occur uniformly. Local regions acquire an older biological phenotype before the surrounding tissue, and these microdomains expand and coalesce with age.

Age progression. 9m: relatively homogeneous predictions close to chronological age. 18m: focal older-predicted microdomains emerge. 21m: these regions become larger and more connected. 25m: old predictions are widespread, although younger-like islands remain.

Features to compute. Predicted-age SD, IQR, MAD, robust CV; fraction of accelerated-aging tubules; old-region area fraction; number and size of connected old clusters; largest old-cluster fraction; Moran’s I; Geary’s C; young-island fraction and size.

Strength. Very strong

2. Loss of epithelial compartmentalization / apical–basal polarity

Hypothesis. Aging disrupts the normal spatial organization of protein and lipid across tubular epithelial cells.

Age progression. Organized at 9m; early disruption around 18m; clear heterogeneous remodeling at 21m; widespread disorganization at 25m.

Features to compute. protein_apical_basal_ratio, lipid_apical_basal_ratio, protein_radial_gradient, lipid_radial_gradient, protein_lipid_radial_correlation.

Expected result. Radial organization weakens or becomes more variable with age.

Strength. Very strong

3. Brush-border deterioration

Hypothesis. The proximal-tubule brush border progressively loses coverage, continuity, and structural integrity during aging.

Age progression. Relatively intact at 9m; early irregularity at 18m; increased fragmentation at 21m; substantial disruption at 25m.

Features to compute. brush_border_coverage, brush_fraction, brush_fragmentation, largest_brush_component_fraction, brush_thickness, optional brush_gap_fraction.

Expected result. Coverage ↓, brush fraction ↓, fragmentation ↑, largest connected component ↓.

Strength. Very strong

4. Tubular degeneration and architectural instability

Hypothesis. Tubular architecture progressively loses organization, producing irregular, deformed, broken, and eventually collapsed structures.

Age progression. 9m organized; 18m early defects; 21m focal structural failure; 25m widespread degeneration.

Features to compute. tubule_radial_roughness_cv, tubule_concavity_fraction, tubule_solidity, epithelial_thickness_cv, tubule_eccentricity, tubule_perimeter_area_ratio.

Expected result. Roughness ↑, concavity ↑, thickness variability ↑, perimeter/area ↑, solidity ↓.

Strength. Very strong

5. Lumen-boundary degradation

Hypothesis. The lumen-facing epithelial surface becomes rougher, more indented, and less regular with age.

Age progression. First clearly apparent around 18m and stronger at 21–25m.

Features to compute. radial_roughness_cv, concavity_fraction, lumen circularity, lumen solidity, invagination count/depth, lumen perimeter-to-area ratio.

Strength. Strong

6. Outer tubule-boundary degradation

Hypothesis. Aging affects the external tubule contour, reflecting epithelial wall deformation and local tissue remodeling.

Age progression. Early changes at 18m; increased deformation at 21m; pronounced irregularity/collapse at 25m.

Features to compute. tubule_radial_roughness_cv, tubule_concavity_fraction, tubule_solidity, tubule_eccentricity, tubule_perimeter_area_ratio, epithelial_thickness_cv.

Strength. Strong

7. Increasing tissue and cellular heterogeneity

Hypothesis. A major property of kidney aging is increasing heterogeneity: neighboring cells and tubules increasingly occupy different biological states rather than changing synchronously.

Age progression. 9m relatively homogeneous; 18m heterogeneity emerges; 21m strong mixture of young-like and old-like structures; 25m old phenotype dominates but local variability persists.

Features to compute. Predicted-age SD/IQR/MAD; robust_cv_protein; robust_cv_lipid; cell-to-cell CV; within-animal feature variance; fraction of accelerated-aging tubules.

Strength. Very strong

8. Localized protein depletion / redistribution

Hypothesis. Aging produces subsets of epithelial cells with reduced cytoplasmic protein signal rather than a simple uniform decline across all cells.

Age progression. Begins around 18m; mixed high/low-protein populations are prominent at 21m; extensive abnormal regions appear at 25m.

Features to compute. Median cytoplasmic protein per cell; number and fraction of protein-depleted cells per tubule; robust_cv_protein; depleted-cell cluster size; largest depleted-cell cluster.

Strength. Strong

9. Protein texture becomes fragmented / granular

Hypothesis. Cytoplasmic protein organization changes from relatively smooth to increasingly fragmented or granular with age.

Age progression. Detectable around 18m and increasingly apparent at 21–25m.

Features to compute. protein_texture_roughness, high-frequency residual intensity, local protein variance, entropy, robust_cv_protein, aggregate/granule number and size.

Strength. Strong

10. Loss of lipid homeostasis / disorganized lipid distribution

Hypothesis. Aging alters lipid organization rather than simply increasing total lipid. Organized lipid distribution becomes increasingly patchy and lesion-associated.

Age progression. 9m organized distribution; 18m early redistribution; 21m lipid clusters/debris; 25m prominent lipid-rich cytoplasm and droplets.

Features to compute. robust_cv_lipid, lipid_texture_roughness, lipid-droplet number, area and size distribution, lipid-rich area fraction, lipid_apical_basal_ratio, lipid_radial_gradient.

Strength. Strong

11. Protein–lipid spatial coordination decreases

Hypothesis. Normal spatial relationships between protein and lipid become less coordinated as epithelial organization deteriorates.

Age progression. Possible early disruption at 18m; strongest expected at 21–25m.

Features to compute. protein_lipid_radial_correlation, within-tubule pixel-level protein/lipid correlation, boundary correlation, spatial cross-correlation.

Strength. Strong, quantitative confirmation still needed.

12. Nephron segments differ in aging susceptibility / heterogeneity

Hypothesis. Proximal, distal, and collecting tubules all undergo aging, but the magnitude, timing, and phenotype differ by segment.

Age progression. Segment differences are most informative from 18–25m.

Features to compute. Predicted biological age by tubule type, prediction error, variance/IQR, accelerated-aging fraction, protein/lipid heterogeneity, brush-border and architecture features by segment, age × tubule-type interaction.

Strength. Strong

13. Collecting tubules contain a strong aging signal

Hypothesis. Collecting tubules contain substantial age-associated information and may show particularly heterogeneous remodeling at intermediate ages.

Age progression. Particularly interesting around 21m.

Features to compute. Median predicted age by tubule type, prediction variance/IQR, fraction predicted older than chronological age, per-animal prediction error, protein/lipid heterogeneity, architecture metrics.

Important limitation. Current data do not establish that collecting tubules globally age faster than proximal or distal tubules.

Strength. Moderate–Strong

14. Glomerular remodeling contributes to biological aging

Hypothesis. Structural and biochemical remodeling of glomeruli contributes to the age-prediction signal.

Age progression. Visible from approximately 18m, with stronger abnormalities at 21–25m.

Features to compute. Glomerular area, circularity, solidity, capillary/lumen fraction, empty-space fraction, protein/lipid levels and heterogeneity, boundary roughness, Grad-CAM overlap.

Strength. Moderate–Strong

15. Vascular remodeling contributes to aging

Hypothesis. Vessel walls and perivascular regions undergo age-related structural and biochemical remodeling.

Age progression. Mainly 21–25m.

Features to compute. Vessel-wall thickness, wall-thickness CV, wall:lumen ratio, vessel protein/lipid intensity, wall heterogeneity, boundary roughness, Grad-CAM vascular overlap.

Strength. Moderate

16. Interstitial / stromal remodeling increases with advanced age

Hypothesis. Advanced aging increasingly involves extratubular tissue rather than being restricted to tubular epithelium.

Age progression. Emerges around 21m and is most obvious at 25m.

Features to compute. Intertubular area fraction, interstitial protein/lipid intensity and robust CV, stromal cluster number/area, distance between tubules, extratubular signal fraction, Grad-CAM attention outside tubules.

Important limitation. Interstitial/stromal remodeling is defensible; fibrosis specifically needs dedicated histological or molecular validation.

Strength. Strong

17. 21 months is a transition / remodeling state

Hypothesis. Twenty-one months represents a transition between localized early aging and the widespread old phenotype observed at 25m.

Features to compute. Predicted-age variance/IQR, young-like and old-like fractions, old-region cluster size/connectivity, spatial autocorrelation, within-sample feature heterogeneity, possible multimodality of predicted-age distributions.

Strength. Very strong

18. Young-like microdomains persist in old kidneys

Hypothesis. Even at 25m, some regions retain morphological and biochemical characteristics associated with a younger biological state.

Age progression. Most evident at 25m.

Features to compute. Fraction of substantially younger-predicted tubules, young-island number and area, largest young component, tubule-type composition, and within-25m comparisons of brush/polarity/protein/lipid/architecture features between young-like and old-like regions.

Strength. Strong

Central working model:
Loss of epithelial polarity and brush-border integrity → increasing protein/lipid heterogeneity → lumen and tubule architectural degradation → expansion and coalescence of locally aged tissue microdomains.

Recommended statistical strategy

Use two complementary analyses. Between chronological ages, test whether features change across 9m, 18m, 21m, and 25m while accounting for animal/sample identity. Within each chronological age, test why tubules from the same age—and preferably the same animal—receive different biological-age predictions.

For within-age analysis, compute feature–prediction associations per animal, visualize top features across predicted-age bins, and test whether the association direction reproduces across independent animals. Mixed-effects models with animal/sample as a random effect are preferable for formal inference. Apply FDR correction and avoid treating many tubules from the same kidney as independent biological replicates.
