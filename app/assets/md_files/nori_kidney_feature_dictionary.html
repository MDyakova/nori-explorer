NoRI Kidney Tubule Feature Dictionary

NoRI Kidney Tubule Feature Dictionary

Purpose

This document describes the variables generated by the supplied kidney-tubule analysis notebooks and stored in the three example tables:

  • Kidney_9mo_M146_MAP1.csv — tubule-level biochemical, compartment, texture, nuclear, lipid-rich-region, and model-associated measurements.
  • Kidney_9mo_M146_MAP1_nucleolus.csv — nucleus-level and perinuclear measurements.
  • Kidney_9mo_M146_MAP1_shape.csv — brush-border, epithelial-polarity, lumen-shape, and tubule-shape measurements.

The definitions below follow the actual implementation in the supplied notebooks. This is important because some variable names are shorthand; for example, protein_delta is not a difference between biological groups but an image-detail/roughness measurement.

1. General notation

For a set of pixels (R), define the mean signal as

[ I_R = _{pR} I(p) ]

where (I(p)) is the protein or lipid NoRI signal at pixel (p).

The main notebook applies calibration factors

[ k_{} = ]

and

[ k_{} = . ]

Thus, most reported biochemical intensity features have the form

[ = I_R k. ]

The precise physical unit therefore depends on the calibration convention used for the input NoRI images.

Anatomical masks

The calculations use several segmentation masks:

  • Tubule mask — pixels belonging to one segmented tubule.
  • Nuclei mask — segmented nuclei inside the tubule.
  • Lumen mask — segmented tubule lumen.
  • Brush-border mask — segmented brush border.
  • Tubule body / epithelium — in the first notebook, usually tubule pixels after excluding nuclei, lumen, and brush border.
  • Peritubular region — a dilated ring outside the selected tubule, excluding other segmented tubules.

2. Main tubule-level table

Identification and bookkeeping columns

class_name

Biological class assigned to the sample. In the aging dataset this is the age group extracted from the filename, e.g. 9, 18, 21, or 25 months.

This is a grouping variable, not a measured biological feature.

file_name_save

Identifier of the source NoRI image/sample map.

tubule_type

Predicted tubule subtype, such as proximal, distal, or collecting tubule.

contour_id

Label of the segmented tubule object in the tubule segmentation mask.

tb_size

Size of the segmented tubule object in pixels at the stage where the contour is processed.

min_x, min_y, max_x, max_y

Bounding-box coordinates surrounding the tubule object. These are localization metadata rather than biological features.

step

Sequential tubule/object index used during processing.

2.1 Tubule-body biochemical features

protein_level

Mean calibrated protein signal in the tubule body after excluding nuclei, lumen, and brush-border pixels.

[ = k_P, ]

where (T) is the tubule mask, (N) nuclei, (L) lumen, and (B) brush border.

Interpretation: overall protein concentration/signal in the non-nuclear epithelial body of the tubule.

Higher values indicate stronger average protein signal in this compartment.

lipid_level

Mean calibrated lipid signal in the same tubule-body region.

[ = k_L, . ]

Interpretation: overall lipid abundance/signal in the non-nuclear epithelial body.

2.2 Nuclear and perinuclear biochemical features

protein_level_nuclei

Mean calibrated protein signal inside segmented nuclei that lie within the tubule.

[ = k_P,[P(p):pTN]. ]

Interpretation: average nuclear protein signal.

protein_level_around_nuclei

Mean calibrated protein signal in a perinuclear ring.

The nuclei mask is dilated using a (3) kernel for five iterations. Pixels inside the original nuclei, lumen, and brush border are excluded.

[ R_{} = TD_5(N)NLB ]

and

[ = k_P,{pR{}}P(p). ]

Interpretation: local protein environment immediately surrounding nuclei, approximately representing perinuclear cytoplasm.

lipid_level_around_nuclei

Mean calibrated lipid signal in the same perinuclear region.

[ = k_L,{pR{}}Lipid(p). ]

Interpretation: local perinuclear lipid environment.

2.3 Lumen features

Before measurement, the lumen mask is morphologically dilated once and eroded once with a (3) kernel.

protein_level_lumen

Mean calibrated protein signal in the processed lumen region inside the tubule, excluding nuclei.

[ = k_P,_{pTL’}P(p). ]

Interpretation: protein signal associated with the lumen/luminal contents.

lipid_level_lumen

Mean calibrated lipid signal in the same processed lumen region.

[ = k_L,_{pTL’}Lipid(p). ]

Interpretation: lipid signal associated with the lumen/luminal contents.

lumen_size

Number of pixels belonging to the processed lumen region inside the tubule.

[ =|TL’|. ]

Interpretation: two-dimensional lumen area in pixels. It can reflect lumen dilation/constriction, but it is not normalized to total tubule size in this column.

2.4 Brush-border intensity and size

protein_level_bb

Mean calibrated protein signal in pixels classified as brush border inside the tubule, excluding nuclei and lumen.

[ = k_P,_{pBT}P(p). ]

Interpretation: protein abundance in the segmented brush-border compartment.

lipid_level_bb

Mean calibrated lipid signal in the brush-border compartment.

[ = k_L,_{pBT}Lipid(p). ]

Interpretation: lipid abundance in the segmented brush border.

bb_size

Number of pixels assigned to brush border inside the tubule.

[ =|BT|. ]

Interpretation: absolute brush-border area in pixels. Because this is not normalized by tubule or epithelial size, larger tubules can naturally have larger values. The shape table contains normalized brush-border measurements that are more suitable for comparing relative brush-border preservation.

2.5 Peritubular / tubule-surrounding features

The tubule mask is dilated with a (7) kernel for three iterations. The measured region consists of dilated pixels that are outside the selected tubule and outside all other segmented tubules.

Let

[ R_{} = D(T)(TT_{}). ]

protein_level_tb_conn

Mean calibrated protein signal in the peritubular region.

[ = k_P,{pR{}}P(p). ]

Interpretation: local protein environment immediately surrounding the tubule. Depending on the image, this region can include interstitium, extracellular space, vessels, or other non-tubular tissue.

lipid_level_tb_conn

Mean calibrated lipid signal in the same peritubular region.

[ = k_L,{pR{}}Lipid(p). ]

tb_conn_size

Number of pixels in the peritubular measurement region.

[ =|R_{}|. ]

This mainly describes the amount of available non-tubular space around the object under the implemented dilation rule.

black_holes

Number of pixels in the peritubular region for which both normalized display channels are below 100:

[ = #{pR_{}: I_P^{segm}(p)<100 I_L^{segm}(p)<100}. ]

Interpretation: amount of very low-signal space around the tubule according to this operational threshold. It may capture empty/background-like or low-density regions, but the notebook does not anatomically classify those pixels, so it should not automatically be interpreted as a specific lesion or structure.

2.6 Nuclear and nucleolar summary features

Only nuclei with contour area (>50) pixels are retained for the final tubule-level nuclear summary.

nuclei_number

Number of unique qualifying nuclei detected within the tubule.

[ =N_{}. ]

Interpretation: nuclear count per segmented tubule. It is not normalized by tubule area.

nuclei_area_mean

Mean contour area of qualifying nuclei.

[ = _{i=1}^{N} A_i. ]

Interpretation: average 2-D nuclear size in pixels².

nucleolus_number_in_nuclei

For each nucleus, nucleoli are predicted by a U-Net and candidate nucleolar contours with area (>5) pixels are counted. The feature is the mean number of accepted nucleoli per qualifying nucleus.

[ = _{i=1}^{N} n_i. ]

Interpretation: average nucleolar multiplicity per nucleus.

nucleolus_area_in_nuclei

Mean nucleolar contour area, calculated after grouping accepted nucleoli by nucleus. Nuclei/nucleoli with positive accepted nucleolar area contribute to the calculation.

Interpretation: characteristic 2-D nucleolar size in pixels² among nuclei containing accepted nucleolar detections.

2.7 Image-detail / heterogeneity features

protein_delta

The tubule body is cropped, downsampled to half its width and height using bilinear interpolation, then resized back to its original dimensions. The absolute difference between the original and blurred/reconstructed image is calculated. protein_delta is the mean absolute difference in the protein channel over tubule-body pixels.

If (P) is the original protein image and (P) is the downsampled-then-upsampled image,

[ = _{pR} |P(p)-P(p)|. ]

Interpretation: local high-spatial-frequency variation or image texture in the protein channel. Higher values mean the protein image contains more fine-scale variation that is lost by smoothing/downsampling.

It should not be described as a protein concentration difference between age groups.

lipid_delta

Same calculation for the lipid channel:

[ = _{pR} |L(p)-L(p)|. ]

Interpretation: fine-scale lipid heterogeneity/texture.

2.8 Lipid-rich pixel features

The implementation identifies candidate lipid-rich pixels inside the tubule body using

[ Lipid(p)>200 ]

and

[ Protein(p)<Lipid(p), ]

while excluding nuclei, lumen, and brush border.

These are threshold-defined lipid-rich pixels; the code does not perform connected-component segmentation of individual lipid droplets. Therefore, the feature name lipid_droplet_size should be interpreted carefully.

lipid_droplet_size

Number of tubule-body pixels satisfying the lipid-rich criterion.

[ = #{pR_{}: Lipid(p)>200,, Protein(p)<Lipid(p)}. ]

Interpretation: total area of threshold-defined lipid-rich regions in pixels, not the mean size of individual droplets.

body_size

Total number of tubule-body pixels after excluding nuclei, lumen, and brush border.

[ =|R_{}|. ]

This is useful as a denominator for constructing a normalized lipid-rich fraction:

[ f_{} = {}. ]

That fraction is not directly stored in the supplied table.

lipid_droplet_level

Mean calibrated lipid signal among the threshold-defined lipid-rich pixels.

[ = k_L, . ]

Interpretation: intensity of lipid signal within candidate lipid-rich regions.

2.9 Model-associated columns

pred_320

Prediction associated with the best-overlapping 320×320 image tile from the aging model output.

This is a model output rather than a hand-engineered biological feature.

protein_attn_320

Protein-channel attention value associated with the selected 320×320 tile.

Interpretation: model-derived relative importance/attention assigned to protein information for that tile. It should not be interpreted as protein abundance.

lipid_attn_320

Lipid-channel attention value associated with the selected 320×320 tile.

pred_128

Prediction associated with the best-overlapping 128×128 tile.

protein_attn_128

Protein attention associated with the selected 128×128 tile.

lipid_attn_128

Lipid attention associated with the selected 128×128 tile.

Attention variables are properties of the trained model and its representation, not direct quantitative biochemical measurements.

3. Nucleus-level / nucleolus table

Each row in this table corresponds to a nucleus–nucleolus record generated during nucleolar analysis. A nucleus with multiple accepted nucleoli can therefore appear in multiple rows.

class_name

Sample class/age group.

file_name_save

Source image identifier.

step

Parent tubule index.

step_n

Index of the nucleus within the parent tubule.

area_n

Contour area of the nucleus in pixels².

nucleolus

Binary indicator used by the notebook:

  • 1 — an accepted nucleolus was detected,
  • 0 — no accepted nucleolus was detected.

Accepted nucleolar contours must have area (>5) pixels.

area_nc

Area of the accepted nucleolar contour in pixels². It is set to 0 when no accepted nucleolus is present.

protein_level_around_nuclei

Mean calibrated protein signal in the perinuclear region surrounding this individual nucleus.

The nucleus is eroded by two iterations for the internal mask construction, then dilated by five iterations with a (3) kernel to define the local neighborhood. Nuclear, lumen, and brush-border pixels are excluded from the measured ring.

lipid_level_around_nuclei

Mean calibrated lipid signal in the same individual-nucleus perinuclear region.

robust_cv_protein

Robust measure of protein-signal heterogeneity in the perinuclear region:

[ = {(P)+10^{-8}}, ]

where

[ (P) = (|P_i-(P)|). ]

In the notebook this is computed with scipy.stats.median_abs_deviation using its default scale.

Interpretation: relative local heterogeneity of perinuclear protein signal. It is analogous in purpose to a coefficient of variation but uses the median and MAD, making it less sensitive to extreme pixels than SD/mean.

Higher values indicate greater protein-signal variability relative to the local median.

robust_cv_lipid

Equivalent robust heterogeneity measure for the perinuclear lipid signal:

[ = {(L)+10^{-8}}. ]

label_id

Label of the parent tubule object in the segmentation mask.

tubule_type

Tubule subtype assigned to the parent tubule.

4. Shape and epithelial-organization table

The shape notebook first retains the largest connected tubule component. The lumen is restricted to that tubule and its largest connected component is retained. The epithelium is defined as

[ E=TL. ]

The brush-border mask is restricted to the epithelium.

Default parameters in the feature function are:

  • lumen-facing brush boundary width = 3 pixels,
  • outer-tubule boundary width = 3 pixels,
  • apical width = 5 pixels,
  • basal width = 5 pixels,
  • radial profile bins = 10,
  • angular bins = 72,
  • minimum brush component size = 5 pixels.

4.1 Brush-border organization

brush_border_coverage

A lumen-facing epithelial ring is generated by dilating the lumen by three pixels and intersecting it with the epithelium.

Let (R_L) be this lumen-facing ring and (B) the brush mask.

[ = {|R_L|}. ]

Range: approximately 0–1.

Interpretation: fraction of the expected lumen-facing epithelial boundary that is occupied by segmented brush border.

  • High value: brush border covers much of the luminal surface.
  • Low value: reduced or discontinuous brush-border coverage.

This is particularly interpretable for proximal tubules, where a prominent brush border is expected.

brush_fraction

Fraction of the entire epithelial area classified as brush border:

[ = {|E|}. ]

Interpretation: relative amount of brush-border tissue compared with total epithelium.

Unlike bb_size, this measurement is normalized for epithelial size.

brush_fragmentation

The brush border lying on the lumen-facing boundary is divided into connected components. Components smaller than 5 pixels are discarded.

[ = {|R_L|} . ]

Unit: accepted brush-border components per 1000 lumen-boundary pixels.

Interpretation: degree to which the brush border is broken into separate fragments.

Higher values generally indicate a more fragmented/discontinuous brush-border pattern, although the value also depends on segmentation quality and the minimum-component threshold.

largest_brush_component_fraction

[ = {A_{}}. ]

Range: 0–1.

Interpretation: dominance of the largest continuous brush-border segment.

  • Near 1: most brush-border pixels belong to one continuous component.
  • Lower value: brush border is distributed among several disconnected fragments.

This complements brush_fragmentation.

brush_thickness

For each brush-border pixel, the Euclidean distance transform gives its distance to the nearest non-brush pixel. The notebook approximates local full thickness as twice this distance:

[ t(p)=2D_B(p). ]

The reported feature is

[ = _{pB}[2D_B(p)]. ]

If pixel_size_um is supplied, the result is converted to µm; otherwise it remains in pixels.

Interpretation: median thickness of the segmented brush-border structures.

4.2 Apical–basal epithelial polarity

A normalized radial epithelial coordinate is created using distance from the lumen and distance from the outer tubule boundary:

[ r(p) = {d_{}(p)+d_{}(p)}. ]

Thus:

  • (r): lumen-facing/apical,
  • (r): outer/basal.

The default apical zone contains epithelial pixels within 5 pixels of the lumen; the basal zone contains epithelial pixels within 5 pixels of the outer tubule boundary. If the two zones overlap in thin epithelium, each overlapping pixel is assigned to the closer boundary.

protein_apical_basal_ratio

[ = {P_{}}. ]

Interpretation:

  • (>1): protein signal enriched toward the lumen/apical side.
  • (<1): protein signal enriched toward the basal side.
  • (): similar average protein signal in apical and basal zones.

This measures spatial polarity, not total protein abundance.

lipid_apical_basal_ratio

[ = {L_{}}. ]

Interpretation is analogous to the protein ratio but for lipid distribution.

4.3 Radial biochemical organization

The normalized epithelial coordinate is divided into 10 equal bins from apical ((r=0)) to basal ((r=1)). Mean protein and lipid signals are calculated in each bin.

protein_radial_gradient

The feature is Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient between radial-bin position and the mean protein profile:

[ _P = (r_j,P_j). ]

Despite the name gradient, this is not a linear slope.

Range: (-1) to (+1).

Interpretation:

  • positive: protein tends to increase from apical toward basal,
  • negative: protein tends to decrease from apical toward basal, i.e. higher apically,
  • near zero: little monotonic apical–basal trend.

lipid_radial_gradient

[ _L = (r_j,L_j). ]

Interpretation is identical for the lipid radial profile.

protein_lipid_radial_correlation

Spearman correlation between the binned protein and lipid radial profiles:

[ _{PL} = (P_j,L_j). ]

Interpretation:

  • positive: protein and lipid tend to be high and low in the same radial epithelial zones,
  • negative: protein-rich radial zones tend to correspond to lipid-poor zones and vice versa,
  • near zero: little monotonic correspondence between their radial profiles.

This describes co-organization across the epithelial radius, not pixelwise protein–lipid correlation.

4.4 Lumen-shape features

The CSV uses the shorter column names radial_roughness_cv and concavity_fraction; in the feature function these correspond to the lumen measurements lumen_radial_roughness_cv and lumen_concavity_fraction.

radial_roughness_cv

For the lumen contour, the object center is estimated from the mean coordinates of lumen pixels. The full angular range is divided into 72 sectors. For each sector, the maximum contour radius is measured:

[ r_= . ]

Missing sectors are circularly interpolated when enough valid sectors are present. Roughness is then

[ = {|(r_)|}. ]

Interpretation: variability of lumen radius around its center.

  • low value: relatively radially uniform lumen,
  • high value: irregular, asymmetric, or rough lumen boundary.

Because this is a CV, it is scale-normalized to first order.

concavity_fraction

A convex hull is constructed around the lumen.

[ = {A_{}}. ]

Range: approximately 0–1.

Interpretation: fraction of the convex hull that is missing because of inward indentations/concavities.

  • 0: fully convex shape,
  • larger values: stronger lumen indentation or irregularity.

4.5 Outer-tubule shape features

tubule_radial_roughness_cv

Same radial-roughness calculation as above, but applied to the outer tubule boundary:

[ = {|(R_)|}. ]

Interpretation: radial irregularity of the overall tubule outline.

tubule_concavity_fraction

[ = {A_{}}. ]

Interpretation: amount of inward indentation of the external tubule shape.

tubule_solidity

[ = {A_{}}. ]

Range: 0–1.

Interpretation: compactness/convexity of the tubule.

  • near 1: compact and nearly convex,
  • lower values: more concave or irregular outline.

For the same binary object and hull definition,

[ = 1-. ]

Therefore tubule_concavity_fraction and tubule_solidity are mathematically redundant transformations of one another in this implementation.

epithelial_thickness_cv

A local epithelial thickness map is constructed as

[ t(p) = d_{}(p)+d_{}(p). ]

Thickness values are sampled at epithelial pixels directly adjacent to the lumen. The feature is

[ = {|(t)|}. ]

Interpretation: relative variability of epithelial thickness around the lumen.

  • low value: relatively uniform epithelial thickness,
  • high value: heterogeneous thickness around the tubule circumference.

This describes variability, not mean epithelial thickness.

tubule_eccentricity

Eccentricity is calculated with skimage.measure.regionprops from an ellipse having the same second moments as the tubule region.

A common representation is

[ e=, ]

where (a) and (b) are the semi-major and semi-minor axes of the equivalent ellipse.

Range: 0–1.

  • 0: circular,
  • approaching 1: increasingly elongated.

Interpretation: tubule elongation in the 2-D section.

tubule_perimeter_area_ratio

[ = {A_{}}. ]

Interpretation: amount of boundary relative to object area.

Higher values can occur for smaller, more elongated, or more irregular shapes. Because perimeter/area still has an inverse-length scale dependence, it is not a purely scale-independent shape descriptor.

6. Practical interpretation by biological category

Global biochemical composition protein_level, lipid_level Does the epithelial body change in overall protein/lipid content?
Nuclear environment protein_level_nuclei, protein_level_around_nuclei, lipid_level_around_nuclei, robust CVs Do nuclei or their local cytoplasmic environments change?
Lumen protein_level_lumen, lipid_level_lumen, lumen_size, lumen roughness/concavity Does lumen content, size, or morphology change?

AI/model outputs predictions and attention columns What does the trained aging model predict or emphasize for overlapping image tiles?

7. Important cautions for statistical interpretation

  1. Pixel-count features are size-dependent.
    lumen_size, bb_size, tb_conn_size, black_holes, lipid_droplet_size, body_size, and to some extent nuclear counts depend on the size of the segmented object and/or available surrounding region. When the biological question concerns relative composition rather than absolute area, normalized ratios may be more interpretable.

  2. lipid_droplet_size is not individual droplet size.
    In the supplied code it is the total number of threshold-defined lipid-rich pixels. Individual droplets are not connected-component labeled and measured separately.

  3. protein_delta and lipid_delta are texture measures.
    They quantify loss of fine image detail after downsampling and reconstruction; they are not direct concentration differences.

  4. protein_radial_gradient and lipid_radial_gradient are Spearman correlations.
    They quantify monotonic radial organization and are not regression slopes.

  5. Attention is not abundance.
    protein_attn_* and lipid_attn_* describe model behavior. A high protein attention value does not mean that the tissue has a high protein concentration.

  6. Shape measurements are based on 2-D sections.
    Eccentricity, area, perimeter, roughness, and concavity can depend on the orientation at which a 3-D tubule is intersected by the imaging plane.

  7. Segmentation quality propagates into the features.
    Brush-border, lumen, nuclear, and tubule-boundary measurements depend directly on their corresponding masks. Apparent biological changes should therefore be checked against segmentation performance, especially when comparing conditions with strong morphological remodeling.

  8. tubule_solidity and tubule_concavity_fraction are redundant in this implementation.
    Since both use the same convex hull, one is exactly (1-) the other (apart from numerical/missing-value effects). They should generally not both be treated as independent predictors in the same statistical model.
