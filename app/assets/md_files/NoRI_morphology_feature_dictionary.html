NoRI Kidney Morphology Feature Dictionary

Definitions • formulas • interpretation • low/high examples

Scope. This document explains the requested NoRI-derived morphology and intensity features. For the newer shape/polarity features, formulas and definitions are taken from the supplied notebook compute_tubule_aging_features implementation. The notebook supplied here does not contain the implementation of several legacy features (for example tb_size, protein_delta, black_holes, area_n). For those, the formula below is an operational interpretation based on the feature name and the masks/channels used in this project; these entries are explicitly marked “implementation not present in supplied notebook” and should be checked against the older extraction code before publication.

Notation

Symbol Meaning
T tubule mask
L lumen mask
E = T \ L epithelial mask
B brush-border mask
N nuclear mask
NC nucleolus mask
P(x) protein-channel intensity at pixel x
D(x) lipid-channel intensity at pixel x
A(M) area / number of pixels in mask M
mean(X|M) mean X within mask M
CV standard deviation / |mean|
Hull(M) convex hull of mask M

1. Basic size and intensity features

tb_size [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Size of the segmented tubule.

Formula: tb_size = A(T). If physical calibration is applied: A(T) × pixel_area.

Example: Low value — small tubule cross-section | High value — large tubule cross-section

Biological / morphological interpretation: Primarily reflects tubule cross-sectional size; compare only after consistent segmentation and scale.

protein_level [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Average protein-channel signal in the analyzed tubule/region.

Formula: protein_level = mean(P | T) (or the project-specific analysis mask).

Example: Low value — weak mean protein signal | High value — strong mean protein signal

Biological / morphological interpretation: Reflects average protein-rich material, not a specific protein.

lipid_level [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Average lipid-channel signal in the analyzed tubule/region.

Formula: lipid_level = mean(D | T) (or the project-specific analysis mask).

Example: Low value — weak mean lipid signal | High value — strong mean lipid signal

Biological / morphological interpretation: Reflects average lipid-rich signal.

protein_level_nuclei [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Mean protein signal inside nuclei.

Formula: protein_level_nuclei = mean(P | N).

Example: Low value — protein-poor nuclei | High value — protein-rich nuclei

Biological / morphological interpretation: Measures nuclear protein-channel intensity.

protein_level_around_nuclei [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Mean protein signal in a perinuclear ring around nuclei.

Formula: protein_level_around_nuclei = mean(P | perinuclear ring).

Example: Low value — weak perinuclear protein | High value — strong perinuclear protein

Biological / morphological interpretation: Sensitive to how the perinuclear ring is constructed.

lipid_level_around_nuclei [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Mean lipid signal in a perinuclear ring around nuclei.

Formula: lipid_level_around_nuclei = mean(D | perinuclear ring).

Example: Low value — weak perinuclear lipid | High value — strong perinuclear lipid

Biological / morphological interpretation: Can reflect lipid enrichment around cells/nuclei.

protein_level_lumen [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Mean protein-channel intensity inside the lumen.

Formula: protein_level_lumen = mean(P | L).

Example: Low value — clear/protein-poor lumen | High value — protein-rich lumen

Biological / morphological interpretation: High values may indicate luminal proteinaceous material, but interpretation depends on image quality and segmentation.

lipid_level_lumen [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Mean lipid-channel intensity inside the lumen.

Formula: lipid_level_lumen = mean(D | L).

Example: Low value — lipid-poor lumen | High value — lipid-rich lumen

Biological / morphological interpretation: Measures lipid-like signal within the lumen.

lumen_size [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Area of the segmented lumen.

Formula: lumen_size = A(L).

Example: Low value — narrow/small lumen | High value — large/dilated lumen

Biological / morphological interpretation: Can reflect lumen dilation or narrowing; absolute size also depends on tubule size.

protein_level_bb [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Mean protein signal within the brush-border mask.

Formula: protein_level_bb = mean(P | B).

Example: Low value — weak brush-border protein | High value — strong brush-border protein

Biological / morphological interpretation: Useful mainly for proximal tubules with a recognizable brush border.

lipid_level_bb [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Mean lipid signal within the brush-border mask.

Formula: lipid_level_bb = mean(D | B).

Example: Low value — weak brush-border lipid | High value — strong brush-border lipid

Biological / morphological interpretation: Describes lipid-channel intensity in the brush-border region.

bb_size [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Area of segmented brush border.

Formula: bb_size = A(B).

Example: Low value — little brush-border area | High value — large brush-border area

Biological / morphological interpretation: Absolute brush-border amount; unlike brush_fraction it is not normalized to epithelium size.

protein_level_tb_conn [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Mean protein signal in the tubule-connection / inter-tubule connection mask used by the legacy pipeline.

Formula: protein_level_tb_conn = mean(P | TB_conn).

Example: Low value — weak signal | High value — strong signal

Biological / morphological interpretation: Exact biological meaning depends on the legacy TB_conn mask definition.

lipid_level_tb_conn [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Mean lipid signal in the tubule-connection / inter-tubule connection mask used by the legacy pipeline.

Formula: lipid_level_tb_conn = mean(D | TB_conn).

Example: Low value — weak signal | High value — strong signal

Biological / morphological interpretation: Exact biological meaning depends on the legacy TB_conn mask definition.

tb_conn_size [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Area of the legacy tubule-connection mask.

Formula: tb_conn_size = A(TB_conn).

Example: Low value — small connection region | High value — large connection region

Biological / morphological interpretation: Requires the original TB_conn segmentation definition.

black_holes [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Amount or fraction of very low-signal holes inside the analyzed tissue/tubule mask.

Formula: Typical operational form: A(low-signal holes) / A(reference mask), or count of such holes.

Example: Low value — few/small dark voids | High value — many/large dark voids

Biological / morphological interpretation: Could represent true low-signal spaces, vacuoles, lumen-like spaces, or imaging/segmentation artifacts depending on implementation.

nuclei_number [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Number of segmented nuclei in the region/tubule.

Formula: nuclei_number = count(connected nuclear objects).

Example: Low value — few nuclei | High value — many nuclei

Biological / morphological interpretation: Raw count depends strongly on tubule/ROI size; a density can be more comparable.

nuclei_area_mean [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Mean area of segmented nuclei.

Formula: nuclei_area_mean = (Σ A(N_i)) / n_nuclei.

Example: Low value — small nuclei on average | High value — large nuclei on average

Biological / morphological interpretation: Can capture nuclear enlargement/shrinkage, but is segmentation-sensitive.

nucleolus_number_in_nuclei [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Number of detected nucleoli within nuclei, often averaged or summed per ROI.

Formula: Typical: Σ_i count(NC inside N_i), or mean count per nucleus.

Example: Low value — few nucleoli | High value — many nucleoli

Biological / morphological interpretation: Confirm whether the original code stores total or mean per nucleus.

nucleolus_area_in_nuclei [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Area occupied by nucleoli inside nuclei, often total or normalized.

Formula: Typical: Σ A(NC ∩ N), optionally / A(N).

Example: Low value — small nucleolar area | High value — large nucleolar area

Biological / morphological interpretation: Confirm normalization in the legacy extractor.

protein_delta [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Protein intensity contrast between two project-defined compartments.

Formula: Typical: mean(P | region 1) − mean(P | region 2).

Example: Low value — small/near-zero contrast | High value — large positive or negative contrast

Biological / morphological interpretation: The exact two regions must be recovered from the legacy code before mechanistic interpretation.

lipid_delta [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Lipid intensity contrast between two project-defined compartments.

Formula: Typical: mean(D | region 1) − mean(D | region 2).

Example: Low value — small/near-zero contrast | High value — large positive or negative contrast

Biological / morphological interpretation: The exact two regions must be recovered from the legacy code.

lipid_droplet_size [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Typical size of segmented lipid droplets.

Formula: Typical: mean or median A(LD_i) across lipid-droplet objects.

Example: Low value — small droplets | High value — large droplets

Biological / morphological interpretation: Distinguishes droplet enlargement from simply having more lipid signal.

body_size [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Area of the legacy cell/tubule body mask.

Formula: body_size = A(body mask).

Example: Low value — small body region | High value — large body region

Biological / morphological interpretation: Exact mask definition is needed for publication.

lipid_droplet_level [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Amount/intensity of lipid-droplet signal.

Formula: Typical: mean(D | LD) and/or A(LD)/A(reference mask).

Example: Low value — few/weak droplets | High value — abundant/intense droplets

Biological / morphological interpretation: Exact implementation determines whether this is intensity, fraction, or both.

bb_size_k [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Normalized brush-border size.

Formula: Likely bb_size_k = A(B) / A(reference compartment), commonly tubule or epithelial area.

Example: Low value — small brush-border fraction | High value — large brush-border fraction

Biological / morphological interpretation: Check the denominator in the legacy code.

lumen_size_k [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Normalized lumen size.

Formula: Likely lumen_size_k = A(L) / A(reference compartment), commonly A(T).

Example: Low value — small lumen fraction | High value — large lumen fraction

Biological / morphological interpretation: If denominator is tubule area, this is conceptually lumen fraction.

area_n [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Nuclear area measure in the legacy pipeline.

Formula: Typical: A(N), ΣA(N_i), or A(N)/A(reference mask).

Example: Low value — low nuclear area | High value — high nuclear area

Biological / morphological interpretation: Exact aggregation/normalization is not available in the supplied notebook.

nucleolus [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Legacy nucleolus feature.

Formula: Typical: count or area of nucleoli detected within nuclei.

Example: Low value — few/small nucleoli | High value — many/large nucleoli

Biological / morphological interpretation: The original extractor is required to distinguish count from area/intensity.

area_nc [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Legacy nucleolus/nuclear-compartment area feature.

Formula: Typical: A(NC), ΣA(NC_i), or a normalized nucleolar area.

Example: Low value — low value | High value — high value

Biological / morphological interpretation: Exact definition is not recoverable from the supplied notebook.

robust_cv_protein [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Robust measure of spatial heterogeneity of protein intensity.

Formula: Typical robust CV = robust_scale(P) / |robust_center(P)|; often MAD-based rather than SD-based.

Example: Low value — uniform protein signal | High value — heterogeneous/patchy protein signal

Biological / morphological interpretation: Unlike ordinary CV, robust CV reduces sensitivity to extreme pixels; exact robust estimator should be confirmed.

robust_cv_lipid [implementation not present in supplied notebook]

What it measures: Robust measure of spatial heterogeneity of lipid intensity.

Formula: Typical robust CV = robust_scale(D) / |robust_center(D)|; often MAD-based rather than SD-based.

Example: Low value — uniform lipid signal | High value — heterogeneous/patchy lipid signal

Biological / morphological interpretation: Exact robust estimator should be confirmed.

2. Brush-border integrity features

brush_border_coverage [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Fraction of the lumen-facing epithelial boundary covered by brush border.

Formula: coverage = A(B ∩ lumen-facing boundary) / A(lumen-facing boundary).

Example: Low value — ≈0: almost no boundary coverage | High value — ≈1: nearly continuous boundary coverage

Biological / morphological interpretation: Higher values indicate more complete brush-border coverage along the lumen-facing surface.

brush_fraction [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Fraction of total epithelial area occupied by brush border.

Formula: brush_fraction = A(B) / A(E).

Example: Low value — 0.05: little epithelium classified as brush border | High value — 0.30: substantial brush-border area

Biological / morphological interpretation: Measures relative brush-border amount, normalized to epithelial size.

brush_fragmentation [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Number of valid connected brush-border components per 1000 lumen-boundary pixels.

Formula: fragmentation = N_valid_components / A(lumen-facing boundary) × 1000.

Example: Low value — low: continuous/few components | High value — high: many separate fragments

Biological / morphological interpretation: Higher values indicate a more fragmented brush-border pattern.

largest_brush_component_fraction [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Fraction of brush-border boundary pixels belonging to the largest connected brush-border component.

Formula: largest_fraction = max(component size) / A(brush on lumen boundary).

Example: Low value — low: brush border split among many fragments | High value — high, near 1: one dominant continuous component

Biological / morphological interpretation: High values suggest continuity; low values suggest fragmentation.

brush_thickness [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Median local thickness of the brush-border mask.

Formula: thickness = median(2 × distance_transform(B)[B]); multiplied by pixel_size_um when supplied.

Example: Low value — small: thin brush border | High value — large: thick brush border

Biological / morphological interpretation: Measures geometric thickness, not signal intensity.

3. Apical–basal polarity and radial signal organization

protein_apical_basal_ratio [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Protein enrichment near the lumen-facing (apical) epithelium relative to the outer (basal) epithelium.

Formula: protein_apical_basal_ratio = mean(P | apical zone) / mean(P | basal zone).

Example: Low value — <1: more basal protein signal | High value — >1: more apical protein signal

Biological / morphological interpretation: 1 means similar apical and basal mean signal.

lipid_apical_basal_ratio [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Lipid enrichment near the apical epithelium relative to the basal epithelium.

Formula: lipid_apical_basal_ratio = mean(D | apical zone) / mean(D | basal zone).

Example: Low value — <1: more basal lipid signal | High value — >1: more apical lipid signal

Biological / morphological interpretation: Quantifies epithelial lipid polarity.

protein_radial_gradient [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Monotonic change in mean protein intensity from apical (0) to basal (1) position.

Formula: Spearman ρ(radial-bin center, protein radial profile).

Example: Low value — negative: protein tends to decrease toward basal side | High value — positive: protein tends to increase toward basal side

Biological / morphological interpretation: Near 0 means little monotonic apical→basal trend; magnitude reflects strength of monotonic organization.

lipid_radial_gradient [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Monotonic change in mean lipid intensity from apical (0) to basal (1) position.

Formula: Spearman ρ(radial-bin center, lipid radial profile).

Example: Low value — negative: lipid decreases toward basal side | High value — positive: lipid increases toward basal side

Biological / morphological interpretation: Near 0 means weak monotonic radial organization.

protein_lipid_radial_correlation [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Similarity between protein and lipid radial profiles across the epithelium.

Formula: Spearman ρ(protein radial profile, lipid radial profile).

Example: Low value — ≈−1: opposite radial organization | High value — ≈+1: matching radial organization

Biological / morphological interpretation: Near 0 indicates little monotonic correspondence between the two radial profiles.

4. Lumen and tubule shape features

radial_roughness_cv [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Variability of lumen radius as a function of angle around its center.

Formula: radial_roughness_cv = SD(r_lumen(θ)) / |mean(r_lumen(θ))| after angular binning/interpolation.

Example: Low value — near 0: smooth, regular lumen | High value — high: uneven/irregular lumen boundary

Biological / morphological interpretation: Scale-normalized boundary roughness; this feature is computed on the lumen.

concavity_fraction [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Fraction of the lumen's convex hull not occupied by the lumen itself.

Formula: concavity_fraction = [A(Hull(L)) − A(L)] / A(Hull(L)) = 1 − lumen solidity.

Example: Low value — 0.02: almost convex/smooth lumen | High value — 0.35: strongly indented lumen

Biological / morphological interpretation: Higher values mean stronger inward indentations and deviation from a convex lumen shape.

tubule_radial_roughness_cv [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Variability of the outer tubule radius across angles.

Formula: tubule_radial_roughness_cv = SD(r_tubule(θ)) / |mean(r_tubule(θ))|.

Example: Low value — near 0: smooth outer contour | High value — high: irregular/wavy outer contour

Biological / morphological interpretation: Captures outer-boundary irregularity independently of absolute tubule size.

tubule_concavity_fraction [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Fraction of the tubule convex hull not occupied by the tubule.

Formula: tubule_concavity_fraction = [A(Hull(T)) − A(T)] / A(Hull(T)) = 1 − tubule_solidity.

Example: Low value — 0.02: nearly convex tubule | High value — 0.35: deeply indented/irregular tubule

Biological / morphological interpretation: Higher values indicate greater concavity of the outer tubule shape.

tubule_solidity [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Fraction of the convex hull occupied by the tubule.

Formula: tubule_solidity = A(T) / A(Hull(T)).

Example: Low value — low: irregular/concave tubule | High value — near 1: compact, convex tubule

Biological / morphological interpretation: In this implementation, tubule_solidity = 1 − tubule_concavity_fraction, so the two contain the same information in opposite directions.

epithelial_thickness_cv [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Relative variability of epithelial thickness around the lumen-facing epithelium.

Formula: epithelial_thickness_cv = SD(thickness values) / |mean(thickness values)|, where thickness ≈ distance_from_lumen + distance_from_outer.

Example: Low value — near 0: uniform epithelial thickness | High value — high: strongly variable epithelial thickness

Biological / morphological interpretation: Higher values indicate heterogeneous thinning/thickening around the tubule.

tubule_eccentricity [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Elongation of the segmented tubule, using region eccentricity.

Formula: eccentricity = focal_distance / major_axis_length (regionprops definition), ranging approximately 0–1.

Example: Low value — ≈0: round tubule | High value — closer to 1: elongated tubule

Biological / morphological interpretation: Measures elongation, not boundary roughness.

tubule_perimeter_area_ratio [exact notebook implementation]

What it measures: Outer tubule perimeter relative to tubule area.

Formula: tubule_perimeter_area_ratio = perimeter(T) / A(T).

Example: Low value — low: compact/smooth and/or large object | High value — high: more boundary per unit area

Biological / morphological interpretation: Can increase with irregularity, fragmentation of contour, or smaller scale; interpret with size and resolution.

5. How to read the features together

Brush-border loss pattern: lower brush_border_coverage, lower brush_fraction, lower largest_brush_component_fraction, and higher brush_fragmentation together support loss/disruption more strongly than any single feature.

Lumen distortion pattern: higher radial_roughness_cv and concavity_fraction indicate a less regular lumen; lumen_size or lumen_size_k adds information about dilation/constriction.

Tubule distortion pattern: higher tubule_radial_roughness_cv, higher tubule_concavity_fraction, lower tubule_solidity, and often higher perimeter/area ratio describe increasingly irregular outer geometry.

Polarity remodeling: apical/basal ratios and radial gradients indicate where protein/lipid signal is redistributed across the epithelial thickness, while protein_lipid_radial_correlation asks whether both channels reorganize in the same radial pattern.

Important implementation note

The newer 18-feature block is directly supported by the supplied notebook. Several legacy variables appear only in the user's requested feature list and are not implemented in that notebook. Their entries above are therefore intentionally labeled as operational interpretations rather than exact code definitions. For a manuscript or grant methods section, the older feature-extraction notebook/script should be checked so the precise masks, thresholds, normalization denominators, and aggregation rules can be inserted.
